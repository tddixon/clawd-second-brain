# How to Get from Bangkok to Krabi: Every Option + Real Costs (Don't Overthink This)

Planning the journey from Bangkok to Krabi is stressing you out more than it should. I know because everyone asks the same questions at our hostel reception: Is the bus really 12 hours? Is the flight worth the extra money? What about the train everyone mentions?

Here's what you actually need to know: There are three realistic options, and which one you pick depends entirely on your budget and how much you value your time. I'm going to give you the real costs, actual travel times, and honest advice about which option makes sense for different situations.

No BS, no sponsored links to sketchy bus companies. Just the info you need to decide and book.

## The Quick Answer (If You're in a Hurry)

**Have the budget? Fly.** It's 1.5 hours, costs $30-80 depending on when you book, and you'll arrive fresh enough to enjoy your first day in Krabi.

**Tight budget and have time? Night bus.** It's $15-25, takes 12 hours overnight, and you'll survive (barely) but save a night's accommodation.

**Want scenic and slow? Train + bus combo.** It's an adventure but takes 15-18 hours total. Only do this if you genuinely enjoy train travel.

Now let's break down each option properly.

## Option 1: Flying Bangkok to Krabi (The Smart Choice)

**Travel Time:** 1 hour 20 minutes (flight only) | 3-4 hours total (including airport time)

**Cost:** 900-2,500 baht ($25-70 USD)

**Frequency:** Multiple flights daily (8-12 flights depending on season)

**Airlines:** AirAsia, Thai Lion Air, Thai Smile, Bangkok Airways, Thai Airways

### The Real Deal

This is the option that makes sense for most people, and I say that as someone who usually chooses the cheapest option. Here's why:

You save an entire day. The bus takes 12 hours - that's half a day gone. The flight takes 3-4 hours door-to-door (including getting to the airport, checking in, flying, and getting from Krabi airport to Ao Nang). That's 8-9 hours you can spend on a beach instead of staring at Thai highways.

**How to Find Cheap Flights:**
- Book 2-4 weeks in advance for best prices
- Tuesday/Wednesday flights are usually cheaper than weekends
- Early morning flights (6-8am) are often the cheapest
- Use Skyscanner or Google Flights to compare - don't book through random travel agencies
- AirAsia and Thai Lion Air have the lowest prices (around 900-1,500 baht if you book ahead)

**Which Bangkok Airport?**
Both Don Mueang (DMK) and Suvarnabhumi (BKK) have flights to Krabi. Budget airlines use Don Mueang, full-service carriers use Suvarnabhumi. It doesn't really matter - both are about 45 minutes to 1 hour from Bangkok city center.

**Getting to the Airport:**
- **Don Mueang:** Airport Rail Link doesn't go here. Take bus A1 or A2 (30 baht), train to Mo Chit then taxi (200 baht total), or Grab (250-400 baht depending on traffic)
- **Suvarnabhumi:** Airport Rail Link to Phaya Thai station (45 baht, 30 mins), then connect to BTS. Or Grab (300-500 baht from city center)

**Getting from Krabi Airport to Ao Nang:**
- Airport shuttle bus: 150 baht per person (official counter in arrivals hall)
- Shared minivan: 150-200 baht per person
- Private taxi: 400-600 baht for the whole car
- Grab: 400-500 baht
- Distance: 30km, takes 30-45 minutes

**Budget Breakdown:**
- Flight: 900-2,500 baht
- Bangkok to Don Mueang: 30-400 baht
- Krabi Airport to Ao Nang: 150-600 baht
- **Total: 1,080-3,500 baht ($30-95 USD)**

**Who Should Fly:**
- You have limited time in Thailand (1-2 weeks total)
- You value comfort and arriving fresh
- You can find a flight under 1,500 baht
- You're traveling with a partner and can split airport transfers
- You're not trying to prove how hardcore you are by taking the bus

**Pro Tip:** Book your hostel in Ao Nang ahead of time. Many hostels offer free airport pickup if you're staying multiple nights. Ask when you book. Even if they charge, it's often cheaper than the official taxi.

## Option 2: Night Bus Bangkok to Krabi (The Budget Choice)

**Travel Time:** 11-13 hours (overnight departure)

**Cost:** 500-800 baht ($14-22 USD)

**Frequency:** Multiple departures nightly (usually 6pm-8pm)

**Companies:** Transport Co (government), Phet Prasert, Air Andaman

### The Honest Truth About the Night Bus

I've taken this bus. Multiple times. I'll tell you exactly what to expect so you can decide if saving $20 is worth it.

**The Good:**
- You save a night's accommodation (leave Bangkok at 7pm, arrive Krabi around 6-7am)
- It's cheap
- VIP buses have decent seats (lie-flat-ish)
- You'll have a story to tell

**The Bad:**
- You won't sleep well. The Thai AC is FREEZING (bring a hoodie, I'm serious)
- Bathroom stops every 2-3 hours (at sketchy roadside stops with squat toilets)
- Some buses play movies/music loudly for the first few hours
- You'll arrive exhausted at 6-7am
- The roads are curvy in parts - motion sickness is real

**The Reality:**
If you've done overnight buses in Asia before, you know the deal. If this is your first one, just know: you'll survive, but you probably won't sleep more than 3-4 hours in chunks. Budget 300-400 baht for a hostel to check in early or nap in the common room when you arrive.

**Which Bus Company to Choose:**

**Transport Co (government bus):**
- Most reliable schedule
- Older buses, less comfortable seats
- 600-700 baht
- Departs from Southern Bus Terminal (Sai Tai Mai)

**Phet Prasert / Air Andaman:**
- Private VIP buses
- Better seats (more recline, footrests)
- 700-800 baht
- Often depart from Khao San Road area (convenient)
- Reputation is decent but book directly, not through travel agencies

**AVOID:**
- Any bus company a Khao San Road travel agency is pushing super hard
- Companies with no website or online reviews
- Buses that are "too cheap" (under 500 baht - you get what you pay for)

**How to Book:**
- **Best:** 12Go Asia (website/app) - shows all companies, real prices, actual departure points
- **Also good:** Direct from company websites
- **Avoid:** Random travel agencies in Khao San Road (markup + potential scams)

**Where Buses Depart in Bangkok:**
- **Southern Bus Terminal (Sai Tai Mai):** Official government buses - take MRT to Bang Khae station, then taxi (20-30 baht)
- **Khao San Road area:** Many private buses pick up here - check exact location when booking
- **Various hotels:** Some companies do hotel pickup (confirm in advance)

**Where You Arrive in Krabi:**
- Krabi Bus Terminal (in Krabi Town, not Ao Nang)
- From there: Songthaew to Ao Nang (60 baht, 30 mins) or taxi (300-400 baht)
- Some buses drop at Ao Nang if you ask the driver (not guaranteed)

**Budget Breakdown:**
- Bus ticket: 500-800 baht
- Getting to bus station in Bangkok: 0-100 baht
- Krabi Bus Terminal to Ao Nang: 60-400 baht
- **Total: 560-1,300 baht ($16-36 USD)**
- But add hostel early check-in or nap: +300-400 baht

**Who Should Take the Bus:**
- You're on a strict backpacker budget
- You've done overnight buses before and don't mind them
- You want to save accommodation for one night
- You're traveling in low season (less traffic, better roads)
- Time isn't your main concern

**Survival Tips:**
- Bring a hoodie or light blanket - Thai bus AC is no joke
- Download movies/music to your phone (WiFi is spotty)
- Bring snacks (dinner stops are iffy)
- Take motion sickness pills if you're prone to it
- Sit in the middle of the bus (less bumpy than back, less AC blasting than front)
- Wear compression socks if you have circulation issues (12 hours sitting is rough)

**Pro Tip:** Book your Ao Nang hostel ahead of time and explain you're arriving on the early bus. Most social hostels will let you store your bag, shower, and hang in the common area until your room is ready (usually 2pm check-in). Some offer early check-in for a small fee (200-300 baht).

## Option 3: Train + Bus Combo (The Scenic Route)

**Travel Time:** 15-18 hours total

**Cost:** 700-1,200 baht ($20-33 USD) depending on train class

**Frequency:** 1-2 trains daily from Bangkok to Surat Thani, then bus to Krabi

### How This Works

There's no direct train to Krabi. You take a train from Bangkok to Surat Thani (12-14 hours), then catch a bus from Surat Thani to Krabi (3-4 hours). It's long. It's an adventure. It's only worth it if you actually enjoy train travel.

**The Train Part (Bangkok to Surat Thani):**
- Departs from Bangkok Hua Lamphong Station (MRT Hua Lamphong)
- Two main trains: afternoon departure (2:35pm) or evening departure (7:15pm)
- 2nd class sleeper berth: 700-900 baht
- 1st class sleeper cabin: 1,200-1,500 baht
- Arrives Surat Thani early morning

**The Bus Part (Surat Thani to Krabi):**
- Buses every 1-2 hours from Surat Thani Bus Terminal
- 3-4 hours, 150-250 baht
- Drop off at Krabi Bus Terminal, then songthaew to Ao Nang

**Why You'd Do This:**
- You love train travel and want the experience
- You're stopping in Surat Thani to visit Khao Sok National Park
- You want to see rural Thailand from the train
- You have lots of time and want to break up the journey

**Why You Wouldn't:**
- It's actually MORE expensive than budget airlines
- It takes longer than the bus
- The hassle of changing from train to bus with luggage
- Surat Thani Bus Terminal is not exactly exciting at 6am

**Budget Breakdown:**
- Train: 700-1,500 baht
- Bus: 150-250 baht
- Surat Thani to Ao Nang: 60-400 baht
- **Total: 910-2,150 baht ($25-60 USD)**

**Pro Tip:** If you're doing the train, consider stopping in Surat Thani for a night and visiting Khao Sok National Park (stunning jungle, elephants, lakes). Makes the long journey way more worthwhile than rushing straight to Krabi.

## Option 4: Driving / Renting a Car (For Reference)

**Travel Time:** 10-12 hours of driving

**Cost:** 1,200-2,000 baht/day rental + 2,000-3,000 baht fuel

You CAN rent a car in Bangkok and drive to Krabi. The route is straightforward (Highway 4 south). But here's why most people don't:
- It's 830km - that's a LONG day of driving
- Thai highways can be stressful if you're not used to them
- You need an International Driving Permit
- You'll pay to drop the car in Krabi (one-way fee: 2,000-5,000 baht)
- Total cost ends up similar to flying

Only makes sense if:
- You're road-tripping and stopping at multiple places (Hua Hin, Chumphon, etc.)
- You're traveling as a group of 4+ and splitting costs
- You really love driving

## What I Actually Recommend

Here's what I tell guests at our hostel:

**If you find a flight under 1,500 baht ($40):** Book it. The time saved is worth it.

**If the cheapest flight is over 2,000 baht ($55):** Consider the night bus if you're on a budget and have done overnight buses before.

**If you're unsure:** Fly. You're on vacation. The extra $20-30 buys you an extra day in Krabi, arriving fresh instead of exhausted. That's worth it.

**If you're stopping in other southern cities:** The train or car might make sense for a multi-stop journey.

## Booking Tips

**When to Book:**
- Flights: 2-4 weeks ahead for best prices
- Buses: 2-7 days ahead (they rarely sell out except major holidays)
- Trains: 1-2 weeks ahead for sleeper berths

**Thai Holidays to Avoid:**
- Songkran (mid-April): Everything is booked and expensive
- Thai New Year / Chinese New Year: Same deal
- Long weekends: Lots of domestic travelers

**Best Booking Platforms:**
- Flights: Skyscanner, Google Flights, direct from airline sites
- Buses: 12Go Asia, BusOnlineTicket
- Trains: 12Go Asia or Thailand Railway website

**Scam Warning:**
Some Khao San Road travel agencies will:
- Show you a low bus price, then add "fees"
- Book you on a bus that doesn't exist
- Put you on a minivan instead of the VIP bus you paid for
- Charge double what the actual ticket costs

Book online or go directly to the bus terminal. Don't trust sketchy agencies just because they're cheap.

## Arriving in Ao Nang: What to Expect

**If you fly (arriving midday):**

**Getting from Krabi Bus Terminal to Ao Nang:**

**What to Do First:**

- Meet other travelers at your hostel

## FAQ: Bangkok to Krabi Transport

**Q: Is the bus or flight safer?**
Both are very safe. Thai domestic flights have excellent safety records. Buses are generally safe too, though night buses carry more risk simply because of the driving hours and road conditions. Statistically, flying is safer, but the bus isn't dangerous.

**Q: Can I stop in Hua Hin or Chumphon on the way?**
If you're driving, yes. Otherwise, no - the buses and flights are direct. If you want to stop in beach towns along the way, you'd need to book separate tickets (Bangkok to Hua Hin, Hua Hin to Chumphon, Chumphon to Krabi, etc.).

**Q: What if I'm going to Koh Phi Phi or Koh Lanta instead of Krabi mainland?**
You still go to Krabi first (either fly or bus), then catch a ferry. From Krabi Town, ferries go to Phi Phi and Lanta. Some bus companies offer bus+ferry combo tickets. Just make sure the ferry connection timing works.

**Q: Is there a direct train to Krabi?**
No. The train goes to Surat Thani, then you take a bus. It's long and honestly not worth it unless you love trains.

**Q: Which is better for solo travelers?**
Doesn't really matter. The bus has slightly more social potential (you might chat with seatmates), but most people just sleep. You'll meet people at your hostel in Ao Nang anyway.
