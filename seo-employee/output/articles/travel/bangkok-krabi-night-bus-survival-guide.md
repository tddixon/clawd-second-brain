# Bangkok to Krabi Night Bus: The Honest Survival Guide

You're looking at that 500 baht night bus to Krabi and thinking: How bad can it be? I'll sleep through it and save money on accommodation!

**Reality check:** The night bus is cheap. It saves you a night's accommodation. But you're not going to sleep well, you'll arrive exhausted at 6am, and you'll immediately understand why everyone says "just fly."

That said, if budget is tight and you've got time, the night bus is doable. Here's exactly what to expect and how to survive it.

## The Actual Deal

**Cost:** 500-800 baht ($14-22)  
**Duration:** 11-13 hours (overnight)  
**Departure:** 6pm-8pm from Bangkok  
**Arrival:** 5am-7am in Krabi Town

**What you're saving:**
- Flight: 900-2,500 baht
- One night accommodation: 300-500 baht
- **Total savings: 800-1,500 baht ($22-42)**

**What it costs you:**
- Sleep (you won't get much)
- Energy (you'll arrive wrecked)
- Comfort (12 hours in a bus seat)

## Which Bus Company to Book

**The decent ones:**

**Transport Co (Government Bus)**
- Most reliable schedule
- Buses are older but maintained
- 600-700 baht
- Departs from Southern Bus Terminal (Sai Tai Mai)
- Least likely to scam you

**Phet Prasert**
- Private VIP buses
- Better seats (more recline, footrests)
- 700-800 baht
- Often depart from Khao San area
- Decent reputation

**AVOID these red flags:**
- Any company a Khao San travel agency is pushing super hard
- Buses under 500 baht (you get what you pay for)
- Companies with no online presence or reviews
- "Tourist buses" with no company name

**How to book:**
- **12Go Asia** (app/website) - Best for comparing options
- **BusOnlineTicket** - Official booking site
- **Direct from company** - If you know which one

**Don't book through random travel agencies** - they mark up prices and sometimes put you on sketchy buses.

## What to Actually Expect (The Unfiltered Version)

### The Bus Itself

**VIP/First Class buses:**
- Seats recline pretty far (not flat, but decent)
- Footrests
- Blanket and pillow provided (quality varies)
- AC (VERY cold, bring a hoodie)
- TV screens playing Thai movies/music
- Bathroom on board (use sparingly, it gets gross)

**Standard buses:**
- Seats barely recline
- No footrest
- Maybe a blanket
- Still freezing AC
- More cramped

### The Journey

**6:00pm-9:00pm: Can't Sleep Yet**
- Bus stops for dinner (20-30 min at a roadside restaurant)
- Food is mediocre (bring snacks)
- Bathroom break
- Back on bus, they play movies LOUD
- People are still settling in, talking

**9:00pm-11:00pm: Trying to Sleep**
- Movies finally end
- Lights go out
- You try to find a comfortable position (good luck)
- Someone's phone is at full brightness
- Baby crying three rows back

**11:00pm-5:00am: The Struggle**
- You drift in and out of sleep
- Wake up every time the bus brakes
- AC is FREEZING (seriously, wear a hoodie)
- Your neck hurts
- Random bathroom stops every 2-3 hours

**5:00am-7:00am: Arrival**
- You're exhausted
- Sunrise through the window
- Bus pulls into Krabi Town Bus Terminal
- You've made it (barely)

### What You'll Actually Experience

**You WILL get cold.** Thai AC on buses is no joke. Bring a hoodie or light jacket. The blanket they give you isn't enough.

**You WON'T sleep well.** Maybe 3-4 hours in chunks. Accept this.

**Your body WILL hurt.** Sitting for 12 hours in one position is rough. Your back, neck, and legs will complain.

**The bathrooms ARE gross.** Squat toilets at roadside stops. Bring toilet paper and hand sanitizer.

**Motion sickness IS a risk.** Mountain roads, tight turns. Take meds if you're prone to it.

## Survival Tips (From Someone Who's Done This)

### Before You Board

**Pack these in your carry-on:**
- Hoodie or jacket (AC is FREEZING, cannot stress this enough)
- Neck pillow (the provided pillow sucks)
- Eye mask (lights, phone screens, etc.)
- Earplugs or headphones (babies, snoring, music)
- Water bottle (1L minimum)
- Snacks (dinner stop food is meh)
- Toilet paper (trust me)
- Hand sanitizer
- Phone charger and power bank
- Something to do if you can't sleep (podcast, book, Netflix downloads)

**Wear:**
- Comfortable clothes (sweatpants, leggings, loose shirt)
- Socks (your feet will get cold)
- Nothing tight or restrictive

### On the Bus

**Claim your spot:**
- Middle of the bus (less bouncy than back, less AC blast than front)
- Aisle seat if you're tall (more leg room)
- Window seat if you want to lean against something

**Drink less water:**
- Bathroom stops are infrequent and the toilets are rough
- But don't dehydrate - find the balance

**Take motion sickness meds BEFORE boarding:**
- Once you're nauseous, it's too late
- Dramamine or equivalent, 30 mins before

**Protect your stuff:**
- Phone and wallet on your person (not in overhead)
- Big bag goes underneath bus (take valuables out first)
- Nothing valuable in seat pocket

### Arriving at 6am in Krabi

**You'll be exhausted. Here's your plan:**

**Option A: Power Through**
- Songthaew to Ao Nang (60 baht, 30 mins)
- Hostel lets you store bags and shower
- Nap in common room (most hostels let you)
- Force yourself to stay awake until normal bedtime
- You'll adjust to the timezone/schedule faster

**Option B: Early Check-in**
- Some hostels offer early check-in for 200-300 baht
- Sleep until 2pm
- Wake up and start your day

**Option C: Coffee and Beach**
- Store bags at hostel
- Shower (hostel common bathrooms)
- Get massive coffee
- Go sit at the beach until you wake up
- Check in at 2pm, nap, then evening activities

**My recommendation: Option A.** Power through the tiredness, you'll adjust faster.

## Is It Worth It?

**You should take the night bus if:**
- Budget is your #1 priority (saving $20-40 matters)
- You've done overnight buses before and tolerate them
- You're young and can handle one rough night
- You're traveling long-term (one bad night doesn't matter as much)

**Just fly if:**
- You can afford the extra $20-40
- This is a short trip (don't waste a day being exhausted)
- You get motion sick easily
- You value comfort and arriving fresh
- You're over 30 and your body can't handle this anymore (no judgment)

**The honest take:** Most people regret the night bus. The $20-40 you save isn't worth arriving exhausted and losing half a day to recovery.

**But** if you're truly on a shoestring budget, it's doable. Thousands of backpackers do it every year.

## The Alternative: Flying

**For comparison:**

**Budget flight (booked ahead):**
- 900-1,500 baht ($25-42)
- 1.5 hours travel time
- Arrive midday, ready to go
- Start enjoying Krabi immediately

**Night bus:**
- 500-800 baht ($14-22)
- 12 hours travel time
- Arrive exhausted at 6am
- Need recovery time

**The difference: $10-20 and 10.5 hours of your life.**

When you break it down, flying makes way more sense for most people. But I get it - when you're broke, every dollar counts.

## FAQ

**Q: Will I actually sleep?**
Maybe 2-4 hours in chunks. Don't expect real sleep.

**Q: Is it safe?**
Yes. Thai buses are generally safe. Keep valuables on you, but violent crime isn't a concern.

**Q: Can I charge my phone?**
Some VIP buses have plugs. Bring a power bank to be safe.

**Q: What if I miss my stop?**
You can't. Krabi is the final stop.

**Q: Is the food at stops included?**
No. You pay separately. It's cheap but mediocre.

## Bottom Line

The Bangkok to Krabi night bus is cheap ($14-22) and saves you one night's accommodation. But you won't sleep well, you'll arrive exhausted, and you'll spend the first day recovering.

**If budget is tight:** Go for it. Pack warm clothes, lower your expectations, and survive.

**If you can afford it:** Just fly. The extra $20-40 is worth arriving fresh and actually enjoying your first day in Krabi.

Either way, you'll have a story. The night bus is a rite of passage for broke backpackers.
