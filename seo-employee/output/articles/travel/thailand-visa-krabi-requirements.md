# Thailand Visa for Krabi: Do You Need One? (2026 Update)

You're booking flights to Krabi and suddenly panicking: Do I need a visa? Do I apply beforehand? What if I want to extend?

**Deep breath.** For most Western tourists, Thailand visa stuff is easier than you think.

Here's exactly what you need to know based on where you're from, how long you're staying, and what happens if you want to extend.

## The Simple Answer

**Most tourists get visa-free entry for 30-60 days** (depending on nationality). You show up, they stamp your passport, you're in.

**You only need to apply for a visa beforehand if:**
- You're staying longer than 60 days
- Your country isn't on the visa-exempt list
- You want to work or volunteer

**For a typical 1-2 week Krabi beach vacation:** You're probably fine showing up with just your passport.

But let's break it down properly.

## Visa-Free Entry (The Easy Option)

### Who Gets It:

**60 days visa-free (as of 2026):**
- USA
- Canada
- UK
- EU countries
- Australia
- New Zealand
- Japan
- South Korea
- (and many others - check latest list)

**30 days visa-free:**
- Some other countries (check if yours qualifies)

**How it works:**
1. Fly to Thailand
2. Go through immigration
3. They stamp your passport
4. You're in for 30-60 days
5. That's it

**No application, no fees, no stress.**

### What You Need at Immigration:

**Required:**
- Passport (valid for at least 6 months)
- Return/onward ticket (proof you're leaving Thailand)
- Accommodation booking (sometimes checked)

**Sometimes asked for (rarely enforced but technically required):**
- Proof of funds (20,000 baht per person or ~$600 USD)
- Travel insurance (increasingly common to ask)

**The reality:** Most people just show passport and get stamped. But have the above ready just in case.

## If You Need a Visa (Tourist Visa)

**You need to apply beforehand if:**
- You want to stay longer than 60 days initially
- Your nationality doesn't get visa-free entry
- You want to enter/exit multiple times (Multiple Entry Tourist Visa)

**Tourist Visa details:**
- Validity: 60 days (can extend once for 30 more days = 90 days total)
- Cost: ~$40 USD (varies by country)
- Application: Through Thai embassy in your home country OR eVisa online
- Processing: 3-10 business days

**How to apply:**
1. Check Thai embassy website in your country
2. Fill out application online (eVisa) or in person
3. Provide: passport photos, proof of funds, travel itinerary
4. Pay fee
5. Wait for approval
6. Print visa or get stamp

**Most people don't need this** for short trips to Krabi.

## Extending Your Stay

### Option 1: Visa Extension (The Official Way)

**If you got 30-60 days visa-free and want to stay longer:**

**How to extend:**
- Go to Thai Immigration Office (in Krabi Town)
- Bring: Passport, TM.7 form (they provide), passport photo, 1,900 baht
- They process same day usually
- Get 30 more days

**Where:** Krabi Immigration Office (Krabi Town, about 30 min from Ao Nang via songthaew)

**When:** Go at least 1 week before your stamp expires (don't wait until last day)

**Total cost:** 1,900 baht (~$53)

**This gives you:** Up to 90 days total in Thailand (60 days visa-free + 30 day extension)

### Option 2: Border Run (The Unofficial Way)

**What it is:**
- Leave Thailand before visa expires
- Cross border to Malaysia/Myanmar/Cambodia
- Come back same day or next day
- Get new stamp for another 30-60 days

**How it works from Krabi:**
- Bus/van to Malaysia border (4-5 hours)
- Cross border, stamp out of Thailand
- Stamp into Malaysia (or just turn around)
- Stamp back into Thailand
- New visa-free stamp

**Cost:** Border run packages ~1,500-2,500 baht (includes transport)

**Pros:**
- Get fresh visa stamp
- Can repeat (sort of - see below)

**Cons:**
- Full day of travel
- Immigration might question if you've done this many times
- Technically for tourism, not living in Thailand
- Getting sketchy with new 2026 rules (they're cracking down)

**The reality:** Border runs used to be common. Now immigration is stricter. Do it once, maybe twice, fine. Do it 6 times? They'll ask questions.

## Visa Rules Nobody Tells You

**Maximum time in Thailand per calendar year:**
Technically unlimited with proper visas/extensions, BUT immigration watches for people living in Thailand on tourist visas.

**If you border run constantly, they'll notice.**
After 2-3 border runs, immigration might:
- Ask about your purpose in Thailand
- Give you shorter stamps (7-14 days instead of 30-60)
- Deny entry

**"Proof of onward travel" requirement:**
You're supposed to have a ticket out of Thailand. Airlines sometimes check before boarding. Have a cheap onward ticket booked (even if you cancel it later).

**Overstay penalties:**
- Overstay 1-40 days: 500 baht per day fine
- Overstay 40-90 days: Banned from Thailand for 1 year
- Overstay 90+ days: Banned for 3+ years

**Don't overstay.** Just extend officially or leave on time.

## Practical Tips

**Check your passport expiration:**
Needs 6+ months validity from entry date. If it expires in 5 months, you might have issues.

**Book refundable onward ticket:**
Airlines sometimes check. Book a cheap bus to Malaysia or flight out, cancel after you land if needed.

**Get travel insurance:**
Increasingly required. Cheap policies available online ($20-40 for 2 weeks).

**Keep documentation handy:**
- Hotel booking printout
- Return ticket printout
- Proof of funds (bank statement or cash)

**Don't stress too much:**
Immigration in Thailand is usually straightforward for tourists. They want you to visit and spend money.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I work on a tourist visa?**
No. You need a work permit and proper visa. Don't get caught working illegally.

**Q: Can I study on a tourist visa?**
Short courses maybe, long-term no. Get an ED visa for proper study.

**Q: What if I lose my passport?**
Go to your embassy immediately. Get emergency passport. Immigration can help with exit stamps.

**Q: Can I get a visa on arrival?**
Some nationalities yes, but most get visa-free entry (better). Check your country.

**Q: Do I need a visa for just layover?**
No, if you're not leaving the airport.
