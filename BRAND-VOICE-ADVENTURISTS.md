# The Adventurists Brand Voice Guide

## Overview

The Adventurists are the world's foremost adventure travel company for people who hate comfort, certainty, and common sense. Their brand voice is:

- **Irreverent and cheeky** — Swearing, sarcasm, and self-deprecation are core to their identity
- **Anti-establishment** — They actively mock traditional travel, marketing-speak, and "adventure" as a watered-down concept
- **Chaos-worshipping** — Breaking down, getting lost, and suffering are celebrated as the point
- **British humor** — Dry wit, understatement, and absurd comparisons
- **Adrenaline-fueled** — Everything is extreme, even when it's clearly terrible

---

## Voice Characteristics

### 1. Tone: Chaotic Good

**What it sounds like:**
- A mad genius explaining why terrible ideas are brilliant
- Your slightly unhinged mate who convinces you to do stupid things
- A travel company that actively discourages comfortable travel

**Examples:**
- "Easily the least sensible thing to do with two weeks"
- "If God had made machines, he probably wouldn't have bothered with a Rickshaw. But he would have been wrong."
- "It's like a jungly, mountainy, deserty velvet glove of adventuring joy"

### 2. Vocabulary: Absurdist Superlatives

**Favorite words and phrases:**
- Chaos, mayhem, pandemonium
- Stupid, idiotic, ridiculous (as compliments)
- Rolling turd, children's toy, cake tin (for vehicles)
- Adventure (used ironically and sincerely)
- Un-route (their anti-plan philosophy)
- Chaos-wrought, chaos-mongering

**Swearing policy:**
- Frequent but strategic use of "shit," "fuck," "bollocks"
- Never gratuitous — always serves humor or emphasis
- "We don't give a fuck if you get the perfect instagram shot"

### 3. Sentence Structure: Chaotic Flow

**Patterns:**
- Long, rambling sentences with multiple clauses
- Sudden short punches for emphasis
- Absurd comparisons and metaphors
- Parenthetical asides that mock their own claims

**Example structure:**
> "Imagine yourself and a couple of mates squashed into a 1 donkeypower rolling cake tin tackling thousands of miles of old school adventure. This then, is the Rickshaw Run. Easily the least sensible thing to do with two weeks."

### 4. Anti-Marketing Marketing

**They actively mock:**
- Marketing-speak ("marketing-shits")
- Instagram culture ("perfect instagram shot")
- Comfort and convenience
- Planning and preparation
- "Adventure" as a commercial buzzword

**Their version of adventure:**
> "Adventure isn't a place or an activity. Adventure is not knowing what will happen to you tomorrow or in the next 10 minutes. Adventure is setting forth in a spirit of ignorance. It's getting lost. It's getting stuck. It's getting in trouble."

### 5. The Philosophy: Suffering as Joy

**Core beliefs expressed in copy:**
- Breakdowns are features, not bugs
- Getting lost is the goal
- Uncomfortable = authentic
- If it's easy, it's not adventure
- The worse the vehicle, the better the adventure

**Vehicle descriptions:**
- "A rolling cake tin"
- "A rolling turd"
- "A 2-wheeled children's toy"
- "The greatest vehicle on the planet" (said unironically about terrible vehicles)

## Writing Patterns

### Pattern 1: The Setup and Punch
Set up something as terrible, then declare it brilliant.

> "Essentially, it's not a very good idea. They're not very fast, they smell, they fall over when you go round corners and break down more often than a 3 year old... But somehow, all this makes them better."

### Pattern 2: The Absurd Comparison
Compare mundane things to extreme experiences.

> "A day's driving feels like you've been sat on by an elephant"
> "Staring down at you like a baby ant in a tinyness competition might be some of the highest mountains on Earth"

### Pattern 3: The Anti-Sell
Tell people why they shouldn't do it, which makes them want to.

> "There's no set route, no back-up and no way of knowing if you're going to make it. The only certainty is that you will get lost, you will get stuck and you will break down."

### Pattern 4: The Rule That Isn't
Golden rules that are really just excuses for chaos.

> "DON'T PLAN & DON'T TAKE A SMART PHONE"

### Pattern 5: Sarcastic Sincerity
Say something clearly false with complete sincerity.

> "If God had made machines, he probably wouldn't have bothered with a Rickshaw. But he would have been wrong."

## Do's and Don'ts

### DO:
- Use self-deprecating humor
- Celebrate failure and breakdowns
- Mock traditional marketing language
- Use British spellings (favour, colour, centre)
- Add parenthetical asides that undermine your own claims
- Compare things to food items (cake tin, sandwich)
- Swear strategically for emphasis
- Make everything sound simultaneously terrible and brilliant

### DON'T:
- Use corporate buzzwords (synergy, streamline, optimize)
- Promise comfort or convenience
- Use exclamation points (let the absurdity speak for itself)
- Write like a traditional travel company
- Use generic adventure language
- Promise safety or certainty
- Sound American (keep the British dryness)

## Before/After Examples

### Example 1: Vehicle Description

**BEFORE (Boring):**
> "The Rickshaw is a three-wheeled vehicle commonly used for transportation in India. It features a small engine and open-air design."

**AFTER (Adventurists):**
> "Kneel before engineering's highest life form. If God had made machines, he probably wouldn't have bothered with a Rickshaw. But he would have been wrong. Essentially, it's not a very good idea. ## Writing Prompts/Templates

### Template 1: The Vehicle Introduction
"[Absurd worship phrase]. If [deity] had made [category], they probably wouldn't have bothered with a [vehicle]. But they would have been wrong. Essentially, it's not a very good idea. [List of terrible features]... But somehow, all this makes them better."

### Template 2: The Anti-Description
"[Over-the-top praise]. [Sudden pivot to reality]. [List of horrors]. [Pivot back to celebration]. [Absurd conclusion]."

### Template 3: The Philosophy Statement
"[Thing being mocked] is a word that has had its meaning stamped into the mud of indifference. It has been boil washed by [target of mockery] into meaning '[boring definition].' But [thing] isn't [boring definition]. [Thing] is [absurd but poetic definition]."

### Template 4: The Rules
"[Number] golden rules should be followed: [RULE IN ALL CAPS] & [RULE IN ALL CAPS]. We don't give a fuck if [thing people care about]. We would rather you [absurd alternative]."

## Application Notes

**When to use this voice:**
- Adventure travel content
- Product descriptions for "terrible" equipment
- Anti-establishment brands
- Anything targeting people who hate comfort
- Content celebrating chaos and uncertainty

**When NOT to use this voice:**
- Corporate B2B (too irreverent)
- Luxury brands (too anti-comfort)
- Safety-critical content (they'd mock the safety warnings)
- American audiences who don't get British sarcasm

**Key insight:**
The Adventurists voice works because it delivers on its promises. They actually do make you suffer, and that suffering is genuinely the point. The voice isn't just marketing — it's an honest reflection of the experience.
